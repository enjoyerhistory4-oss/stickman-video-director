# Story Bible — STAY INSIDE

> Step 1 of the series: story only. No script yet (that's step 2).

---

## 1. Series Title

**STAY INSIDE**

Tagline: *Your phone says don't leave. Your fridge says you have to.*

---

## 2. Premise

A zombie apocalypse. You are home alone when your phone screams an emergency alert:

> **STAY INSIDE. DO NOT LEAVE.**

So you stay. But every meal you eat brings you closer to the day you'll have to go out. Food and supplies are the countdown clock.

**Point of view rules (locked):**

- Told in **second person** — the narrator always says "you."
- **You are never shown.** The camera is your eyes. We only see **your hands** and **your phone**.
- Everything you know about the outside comes from your phone, your windows, your door, and the sounds through your walls.

---

## 3. Main Characters

Every character is a basic stickman: round head, line body, line limbs. Each one has **one simple identifying mark** so it reads instantly at small sizes.

### YOU (the viewer)
- **Never drawn as a full figure.** Only two stick hands (lines with simple 4-line fingers) at the bottom of the frame, and the phone.
- **Mark:** a thin **watch/hair-tie on the left wrist**, so the hands are always recognizably yours.
- Personality comes through the hands: shaking, counting cans, typing fast, hesitating on a doorknob.

### THE PHONE
- A rounded rectangle held in your hands. Treated like a character.
- **Mark:** a **cracked top-right corner** and a **battery icon** that is always visible.
- Voice: emergency alerts (all caps, red banner), group-chat messages, news notifications, and the flashlight.
- The battery is a second, smaller countdown clock.

### MRS. OKAFOR — the neighbor across the street
- Older stickman with a **round bun** on top of her head.
- Seen only through your window, in her own window. Communicates with **hand-written signs** held to the glass.
- Calm, practical, has a garden on her balcony. Your first ally.

### DEV — the group-chat friend
- Never seen in person. Exists as **chat bubbles** and a **profile icon: stickman head with a backwards cap**.
- Jokes too much when scared. Lives across town. Messages get fewer as the series goes on.

### THE KID UPSTAIRS (MILO)
- Small stickman, **spiky hair (3 lines)**. Heard first (footsteps, a bouncing ball through the ceiling), seen later.
- Also home alone. Becomes the reason you can't just think about yourself.

### THE ZOMBIES ("the Slow Ones")
- Stickmen with **slumped posture, one arm hanging lower, and X eyes**.
- Drawn in **grey** while everyone else is black, so they read as "wrong" at a glance.
- No names, no faces beyond the X eyes. Mostly seen as **silhouettes**, shadows under the door, or shapes in the street.

### THE VOICE ON THE RADIO (optional, later episodes)
- Never seen. Shown as a **small wind-up radio** with sound-wave lines.
- Broadcasts rescue info that may or may not be true.

---

## 4. World Rules

These rules never change. If the script breaks one, it's wrong.

### Zombies
1. **They hunt by sound.** Noise is the biggest danger. Whispering, silent phone mode, and soft footsteps matter.
2. **They are slow alone, dangerous in groups.** One can be avoided. A crowd (a "drift") cannot.
3. **They can't open doors or climb stairs well.** Locked doors and upper floors are relatively safe. Glass can break.
4. **They're drawn to light at night.** Lights off after dark. Phone brightness all the way down.
5. **A bite turns you within hours.** No cure in this series.

### Home
6. **Inside is safe — for now.** Safety lasts as long as supplies, quiet, and the door hold.
7. **The water still runs, at first.** Then pressure drops. Then it stops (a key turning point).
8. **The power goes out partway through.** After that, the phone battery is everything.

### Phone
9. **Alerts are official but vague.** They tell you what to do, never why.
10. **Signal comes and goes.** Messages can arrive late or out of order.
11. **Battery is finite.** Once the power's out, every screen-on moment costs something. Show the percentage.

### The Countdown Clock
12. **Every episode opens on the supply count.** Food in days, water in liters, battery in percent.
13. **Numbers only go down unless you take a risk.** Supplies only go up by doing something dangerous (going to a neighbor, the hallway, the street).
14. **No magic restocks.** If something is found, the audience should've been able to guess it existed.

### Storytelling
15. **Second person, always.** "You hear it." Never "I" or "they" for the viewer.
16. **The viewer's face, body, and voice are never shown or heard.** Only hands and phone.
17. **Tension comes from sound and waiting**, not gore. Keep it PG-13: implied danger, no graphic wounds.

## 5. Episode List

Each episode = one beat, one supply change. Counts at the start of each episode are the **countdown clock**.

| # | Title | Clock at start | One-line beat |
|---|-------|----------------|---------------|
| 1 | **The Alert** | Food: ~9 days · Water: running · Battery: 100% (plugged in) | Your phone blares "STAY INSIDE. DO NOT LEAVE." — you lock the door, peek through the blinds, and see your first Slow One shuffle down the street. |
| 2 | **Inventory** | Food: 9 days | You empty every cupboard onto the floor and count it out loud: cans, rice, crackers, one sad onion — and realize "9 days" is generous. |
| 3 | **The Sign in the Window** | Food: 8 days | Across the street, Mrs. Okafor holds up a hand-written sign: "YOU OK?" — you write back on a pizza box, and you're not alone anymore. |
| 4 | **Footsteps Upstairs** | Food: 7 days | A ball bounces on the ceiling every night until you realize it's a signal: there's a kid up there, and he's by himself. |
| 5 | **Lights Out** | Food: 6 days · Battery: 100% → dropping | The power dies mid-sentence of Dev's voice message; the fridge goes quiet and you have to eat the cold stuff first or lose it. |
| 6 | **The Last Drop** | Food: 5 days · Water: stopping | The tap coughs and stops — you fill every pot, bottle, and the bathtub with what's left before the pressure is gone. |
| 7 | **Dev Goes Quiet** | Food: 4 days · Battery: ~40% | Dev's messages get shorter, then stop at "going for the store, wish me luck" — and two grey checkmarks never turn blue. |
| 8 | **The Hallway** | Food: 3 days | Your first time opening the door: a silent trip up the stairwell to Milo's apartment with half your crackers, and a Slow One one floor below. |
| 9 | **Two Mouths** | Food: 2 days (now shared) · Battery: ~20% | Milo moves in with you; the food splits in half, and Mrs. Okafor's newest sign shows a drawing of her balcony garden and an arrow: "COME." |
| 10 | **The Radio** | Food: 1 day · Battery: ~8% | The wind-up radio crackles: a rescue point, two days' walk away — and you have one day of food and one dead phone left. |
| 11 | **Crossing the Street** | Food: 0 · Battery: 3% | You and Milo make the run across the street to Mrs. Okafor's at dawn, the phone's last flashlight burst guiding you, while the drift turns toward the sound. |
| 12 | **Stay Inside** | Food: refilled (garden) · Battery: dead | Safe at Mrs. Okafor's, the phone finally dies in your hand — and on its last flicker, a new alert: "STAY INSIDE." This time you're not alone, so you listen. *(Season end; door left open for Season 2: the walk to the rescue point.)* |

**Escalation map:**
- Eps 1–4: Learning the rules, meeting the cast. Low risk, slow drain.
- Eps 5–7: Losses (power, water, Dev). The clock speeds up.
- Eps 8–11: You have to go out. Every gain costs a risk.
- Ep 12: A small win and a reset.
